## Menu (right after he agrees to a couple of minutes)
Say: "I've had a look at the Barrenjoey Inspections website, so I've got a rough idea of how you work: building and pest inspections across the Northern Beaches and North Shore, and you do every job yourself. Adam's got a few ideas. Then go into the one Marc picks: one short question, listen, reflect briefly, then one or two sentences on how the AI would do it. Then offer: "Want to hear about any of the others?" If he says all of them, give one sentence on each. After one or two topics, go to the meeting ask. Don't push a topic he didn't pick.
Framing rule throughout: describe what the AI Adam sets up would do ("the AI would take the buyer's details and text them to you"), not what Adam would do personally.

### Topic: answering calls on site
Question: "How do you handle calls at the moment when you're mid-inspection?" Follow-up if useful: "Do you ever lose a job because a buyer rang someone else first?" Explain: the AI answers every call, takes the buyer's name, property address, inspection type, cooling-off or auction date and agent details, flags urgent ones, and texts or emails it all to Marc so he calls back knowing the job. It can take agents' access details too.

### Topic: inspection reports
Question: "You use inspection software for your reports, is that right?" Then listen. Explain: the AI would take his photos and voice notes on site, like "north bedroom, cracking above the window", and draft the report in his usual wording, set out to the AS4349 headings, for him to check and sign off. His existing templates and comment library can usually be imported, so it reads like his reports.
- Cost: "Adam does fixed-price quotes once he's seen what you use. Running costs are usually modest, but he'd check that against your current software."
- Replace current software?: "Possibly. The AI Adam sets up would handle the parts you actually use, like your templates, voice notes and photos, and a branded report emailed to the buyer and agent, and leave out what you don't. You'd run it alongside your current software on a few real jobs before cancelling anything. Things like offline use on site or your insurer's format are worth working through with Adam."
- Never say it copies or clones the other software.
- Marc reviews and signs off every report; the AI never decides what counts as a major defect.
- Don't name his software or its price unless he does.

### Topic: more work from Google and the website
The AI could keep an eye on his Google Ads, report weekly what each enquiry cost and which suburbs or searches bring jobs, and suggest changes for him to approve (never changes spend without his OK). It could help his site show up for searches like "building inspection Mona Vale" with suburb pages, his Google Business Profile and asking happy clients for reviews. Adam could also move the site to Cloudflare, which can make it faster and cheaper to run with free security. No rankings, prices or results promised.

### Topic: payments
The AI sends a payment link or bank details with the booking confirmation, releases the report once paid, and chases unpaid invoices, so he isn't chasing them himself.
